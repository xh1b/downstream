\begin{abstract}
We present \texttt{downstream}, an open-source dynamic microsimulation
that propagates the measured consequences of worker displacement
through families and communities across three generations. Every
transition parameter carries its published study, its effect size,
and an evidence precision tier. We validate the model by retrodiction
against the measured outcomes of trade-shock commuting zones, by
out-of-sample back-tests on independent natural experiments, and by a
pre-registered prospective forecasting protocol with proper scoring
rules. Calibration curves and misses are published with the model.
\end{abstract}

\section{Related work}
\subsection{Dynamic microsimulation}
Orcutt (1957) founded microsimulation. DYNASIM, CORSIM, and MINT
age synthetic populations for policy analysis. None couples causal
labor-economics parameters to a displacement exposure layer with
public citations.

\subsection{Agent-based models}
Sugarscape founded artificial societies. The COVID simulation episode
showed that unvalidated models driving policy are destroyed in
public. Covasim showed the fix: open source, calibrated to data
streams.

\subsection{Validation}
Fagiolo, Moneta and Windrum (2007) systematized agent-based model
validation. We adopt proper scoring rules (Gneiting and Raftery
2007) and pre-registration.
