\begin{abstract}
[FILLED — revise after V1 lands. Current draft:]
We present \texttt{downstream}, an open-source microsimulation that
propagates the measured consequences of worker displacement through
workers, children, families, and communities across three generations.
Every transition parameter carries its published study, its effect
size, and an evidence precision tier; the model refuses to compute
what its citations do not license, and refuses to publish a number it
cannot walk back to a study. Uncertainty is propagated by Latin
Hypercube sampling in the correct space for each unit, decomposed by
Sobol indices, and reduced by a value-of-information ranking that
tells the bibliography where the next full-text extraction buys the
most precision. We validate by retrodiction against measured outcomes
of China-shock commuting zones, by out-of-sample back-tests on
independent natural experiments, and by pre-registered prospective
forecasts graded with proper scoring rules. Calibration curves and
misses are published with the model.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{Dynamic microsimulation}
Orcutt (1957) founded microsimulation. DYNASIM, CORSIM, and MINT age
synthetic populations for policy analysis. None couples causal
labor-economics parameters to a displacement exposure layer with
public citations.
% Add: the coupling direction. Those models forecast totals; this
% model composes measured causal links. One paragraph, then stop.

\subsection{Agent-based models}
Sugarscape founded artificial societies. The COVID simulation episode
showed that unvalidated models driving policy are destroyed in
public. Covasim showed the fix: open source, calibrated to data
streams.

\subsection{Validation}
Fagiolo, Moneta and Windrum (2007) systematized agent-based model
validation. We adopt proper scoring rules (Gneiting and Raftery
2007) and pre-registration.
